Crazy Factor

Crazy Factor is a shedding game for 2-5 players that flips between Crazy Eights rules and rules based on factors and multiples rather than pip values and suits - "Factor" rules. Each set of rules has a "magic card" that causes a flip to the other set of rules. What is a legal card depends on which set of rules are in force at the time.

Factor rules Crazy Eights rules
You can put down any card that is a multiple or factor of the exposed card, e.g. if 10 (suit doesn’t matter) is the top card on the discard pile, you could play any Ace (1), or 5, or 10, as they are the only factors of 10 in the deck. Jacks are worth 11, and Queens are worth 12. You can put down any card that is the same suit or the same value, e.g. if 10 Clubs is the top card on the discard pile, you could play any Club, or any 10.
Notwithstanding the above, you can play a King on any card. This causes the game to flip to Crazy Eights rules. The player who played the card declares what suit the card is meant to be, e.g. "Crazy Eights - hearts". But since Crazy Eights rules also allow a card of a different suit but the same pip value, it would also be legal to place another King of any suit. Notwithstanding the above, you can play an 8 on any card. This causes the game to flip to Factor rules. The player who played the card declares what pip value the card is meant to be, e.g. "Factor - Queen". You'd choose something either good for you or likely to make it hard for your opponent (e.g. a 7 or Jack).

How to play

  • Deal the same number of cards to each player. (If playing with 4 or 5 players, you might want to shuffle two decks together.) We typically deal 11 cards for two players.
  • Put the rest of the deck face down where everyone can reach it. This is the stock.
  • The rule in play is not defined at the start of the game. Take the top card from the stock, and put it face up next to the stock. This is the discard pile. If it is an 8 or a King, replace it in the deck and draw another card.
  • Players take turns either placing a card on the discard pile (if it is a legal card), or taking the top card from the stock.
  • While rules are ambiguous, i.e. not yet set, players can play any card that is legal under BOTH sets of rules. As soon as you play the first card that is legal under only ONE set of rules, that set of rules is in play until it changes (see below).
  • If the stock runs out, take the top card off the discard pile, shuffle the rest of the discard pile, and put it face down as a fresh stock. (Alternatively, you could end the game there and call it a draw, but we never play that way.)
  • The first player to run out of cards in their hand is the winner. We don’t bother scoring points off what cards the losers have.

Sample starts

First card turned over is a Queen of Spades. The first player could keep things ambiguous by playing any Queen, or the Ace, 2, 4, or 6 of Spades (these would all be legal under both sets of rules), or force rules to Crazy Eights with a King, or any Spade not mentioned above, or force rules to Factor with an 8, or an Ace, 2, 4, or 6 that is not a Spade. We sometimes can play half a dozen (or more) cards under an ambiguous rule set before someone is forced to flip the rules one way or the other.

Note: if the rules have not yet been set, and a potentially wild card is played, if it's still a legal card under both sets of rules, the situation remains ambiguous. For example, if the 4 of spades is in play, you could play an 8 of spades and keep things ambiguous, but if you played an 8 of hearts (changing the suit), that would flip the rules to Factor, and if you played the King of spades (keeping the suit but not being a factor), that would flip the rules to Crazy Eights.

Hint: if you see that your opponent has only one card left, and the only legal card you can play will obviously allow your opponent to win (e.g. playing an Ace under Factor rules), pick up a card rather than play your card. You don't have to play a card, even if it's the only legal card in your hand.
